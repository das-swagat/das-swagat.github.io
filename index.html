My Story

Most work in artificial intelligence begins in controlled settings. Mine did not.

I began in electrical engineering and energy systems, working with physical infrastructure where performance had to be demonstrated beyond simulation. Years of research, teaching, and laboratory development shaped how I approach AI today. If a system does not hold up outside the lab, it is not finished.

I am now a PhD student and Graduate Research Assistant at Virginia Commonwealth University. My research spans computer vision, LiDAR and 3D point clouds, edge-based video analytics, self-supervised anomaly detection, generative medical imaging, and cybersecurity for industrial cyber-physical systems.

A growing part of my work focuses on human-supervised agentic LLM workflows and reproducible smart-manufacturing test environments. I am interested in systems that can reason over structured context, use tools within defined boundaries, preserve evidence, and keep people involved in consequential decisions.

I also care about human-AI collaboration, explainable AI, and responsible AI. In many of the systems I study, trust, oversight, and accountability matter as much as model performance.

Before beginning my PhD, I spent more than seven years at KIIT University as an Assistant Professor and later as Assistant Director for Quality Assurance. Outside research, I enjoy sports analytics, travel, new cuisines, and learning from people with different backgrounds.
